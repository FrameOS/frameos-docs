The **Spectra 6 Inky Frame 7.3"** (August 2025 onwards) replaces the seven-color ACeP panel with
a **Spectra 6** one - deeper, more saturated colors and a much better black - on a Raspberry Pi
Pico 2 W. You can tell it apart from the older
[ACeP version](/devices/pimoroni-inky-frame-7-3-pico2) by the black border along the top of the
panel.

FrameOS runs it as a [thin client](/guide/pico): the [backend](/guide/backend) renders and dithers
the scene to the Spectra 6 palette, and the Pico streams the packed panel bytes into the
controller. The backend has to be reachable when the frame renders.

## Setup

1. Add a frame in the backend with **Flash embedded device** and the
   **Pimoroni Inky Frame 7.3" Spectra 6** preset.
2. In its deploy drawer, **Download firmware (.uf2)** - or take `frameos-<version>-pico-2w.uf2`
   (note the **2w**) from the [latest release](https://github.com/FrameOS/frameos/releases).
3. Hold **BOOTSEL** while plugging in USB and copy the file onto the `RP2350` drive.
4. **Connect over USB → Set up as this frame** in the drawer - see the [Pico guide](/guide/pico).

The panel sits upside down on this board; the preset turns every picture for it, so leave the
frame's rotation at 0 for upright. The five front buttons (A to E) reach your scenes as button
events.

FrameOS ships several Spectra 6 palettes, so you can tune how saturated the render looks against
your particular panel.

## Status

🟢 **Confirmed working** (FrameOS 2026.10.19): USB setup from the deploy drawer, the setup screen,
rendering, the unchanged-frame skip, rotation, the front buttons and scene events are verified on
this board. Battery sleep and an `https://` backend haven't been run on it yet - tell us on
[GitHub](https://github.com/FrameOS/frameos/issues/new) or [Discord](https://discord.gg/9dT9y7EzUw)
how yours does.
