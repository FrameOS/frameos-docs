[flash.frameos.net](https://flash.frameos.net) writes FrameOS onto an [ESP32](/guide/esp32) board
over USB, straight from your browser. It is a page of [FrameOS Cloud](/guide/cloud), but it needs
no account and no sign-in. There are three ways in:

- **[A standalone ESP32](#standalone)**: pick the board, give it a name and your WiFi, and it runs
  on its own - no backend, no account.
- **[From a self-hosted backend](#backend)** on plain `http://`: the backend's USB buttons open the
  flasher in a popup and hand it the frame's setup.
- **[From a setup file](#setup-file)** the backend saved, on whichever computer the board is
  plugged into.

## What you need

- **Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer.** The page talks to the board over Web Serial.
- **A USB cable that carries data.** A charge-only cable powers the board but shows no port.
- **On macOS, WCH's serial driver for a Seeed reTerminal.** Its USB-C port goes to a CH340
  bridge that macOS doesn't recognise out of the box
  ([the steps](/devices/seeed-reterminal-e1004#macos-install-the-wch-serial-driver-first)).

ESP32-C3 boards (the TRMNL OG and BWRY, the XTEINK X4, the XIAO 7.5" ePaper Panel, the 0.42"
OLED) and the [Pimoroni Inky Frames](/guide/pico) are thin clients: they need a
[backend](/guide/backend) to render for them, so the standalone flasher doesn't offer them.

### Set it up

1. Open [flash.frameos.net](https://flash.frameos.net) and pick the board.
2. Give the frame a **name** and your **WiFi**. On a touch board WiFi is optional: the frame's own
   settings page joins a network too - swipe down from the top, then tap the gear.
3. Check the **time zone**. It is taken from your browser.
4. Pick the **starter scenes**. The on-frame **Scene store** and a **Clock** are ticked already;
   tick any of the other samples you want as well.
5. Plug the board in, click **Flash & set up**, and pick the board's USB port.

### What happens

The page reads the chip and its flash size, and downloads the signed release image for that chip
and flash layout. It checks the image's [signature](#signatures) in the browser and writes it to
the board.

Then it waits for the first boot. A brand-new board formats its storage on first boot, which takes
about four minutes on a 32&nbsp;MB board - leave it plugged in. Once FrameOS is up, the page sends
the frame's settings over the USB console, uploads the scenes, and the board joins your WiFi.

### Afterwards

The frame renders its scenes itself. To add more, open the **Scene store** scene on the frame and
install from there - by touch, or with the board's buttons on e-paper
([the store on the frame](/guide/scenes#frame-apps)).

A standalone ESP32 has no admin web page, and it doesn't update over the air. Its settings come
over USB from the flasher, a touch board can change WiFi on its own screen, and a new release goes
on with **Update firmware** in the [board tools](#board-tools).

To hand the frame to a [self-hosted backend](/guide/backend) or [FrameOS Cloud](/guide/cloud)
later, flash it from there.

## Board tools [#board-tools]

The page keeps a few tools for a board on its cable - right after a flash, or when you plug the
board in again later:

- **USB log** - what the board prints, as it prints it.
- **Change Wi-Fi** - another network or password.
- **Update firmware** - writes the current release and keeps the frame's settings and scenes.
- **Restart**.

## From a self-hosted backend [#backend]

Browsers only offer Web Serial on `https://` and on `localhost`. A [backend](/guide/backend)
reached over plain `http://` from another machine - `http://192.168.1.10:8989`, say, or Home
Assistant at `http://homeassistant.local:8123` - can't talk to the board itself. Its USB buttons
open flash.frameos.net in a popup instead: **Flash FrameOS & set up this frame**,
**Update over USB**, **Apply frame settings**, and the settings step of a [Pico](/guide/pico).

- The backend hands the popup this frame's setup - its settings, WiFi and scenes - and the
  firmware image it downloaded itself.
- The flasher shows which backend asked and what it will write. Nothing happens until you click
  and pick the port.
- Progress and the USB log stream back into the backend's **Logs** panel. Keep the backend's tab
  open while it runs.
- Nothing about the frame is sent to FrameOS Cloud.

A backend reached over `https://` or on `localhost` flashes the board itself, with no popup.

## From a setup file [#setup-file]

The frame's USB card in the backend also has **Download setup file**. It saves
`<frame name>.frameos-setup.json`: the frame's settings, WiFi and scenes in one file. Drop it onto
flash.frameos.net to flash or set up that frame from any computer - when the backend runs on a
headless box and the board is plugged into your laptop, for instance.

<Callout type="warn" title="Keep the setup file private">
  The file holds the frame's API key and your WiFi password. Don't share it, and delete it once
  the frame is set up.
</Callout>

## Signatures [#signatures]

Every image the flasher writes is checked against the FrameOS release signing key, wherever it came
from: downloaded by the page, or handed over by a backend. It must be signed as that exact release
and that board layout. The flasher refuses anything else, so it writes nothing but published
FrameOS [releases](https://github.com/FrameOS/frameos/releases).

## Privacy [#privacy]

- There is no account, and nothing to sign in to.
- The firmware download is anonymous.
- The frame's settings and your WiFi go from the page to the board over the USB cable.
- From a backend, nothing about the frame reaches FrameOS Cloud: the setup and the image come from
  the backend, and the progress goes back to it.

## Troubleshooting [#troubleshooting]

- **The port is missing or busy.** Only one program can hold a serial port. Close anything else
  that has it open - a serial monitor, the Arduino IDE, `idf.py monitor`, another flasher tab -
  and try again. Check the cable carries data, and on macOS that a reTerminal's CH340 driver is
  installed.
- **The board stays in download mode.** A board stuck in its bootloader doesn't start FrameOS by
  itself: press its **RESET** button.
- **The first boot takes minutes.** A brand-new board formats its storage on first boot, about
  four minutes on a 32&nbsp;MB board. The flasher waits for it; don't unplug the board.
- **Your board isn't listed.** ESP32-C3 boards and the Inky Frames need a backend to render for
  them. Any other ESP32-S3 with PSRAM can be set up as **Other ESP32-S3 board**.
