Every FrameOS board with keys works the same way. A key plays one of five roles, and each role
has a **press** and a **hold**, nothing else. There are no double or triple presses: pressing a
key twice is two presses, so the same digit twice on a calculator works, and Next twice moves
two steps. A press counts the moment the key comes back up.

A hold acts after one second, while you're still holding the key, with a short beep on boards
with a buzzer. You never have to guess whether you held it long enough.

## The five keys

| Key | Press | Hold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Action** | the scene's: pushes the highlighted button on a screen with buttons, redraws a scene with nothing to press | [voice chat](/guide/esp32#voice-chat) on a board with a microphone, otherwise **Home** |
| **Next / Prev** | the scene's: moves to the next or previous thing to press; on a scene with nothing to press (a photo frame), the next or previous photo or page | the next / previous scene |
| **Back** | back: closes the keyboard or a list first, then goes back in the scene (the page or photo before), and closes the scene list or the settings | **Home** |
| **Home** | the scene list; press again to close it | the scene's settings |
| **Screen** | turns the screen off and on (LCDs) | voice chat if it's the board's only way to talk, otherwise **Home** |

**Home** is the [scene list](/guide/scenes#input): every scene on the frame as a tile. From it:

- **Next / Prev** walk the tiles and **Action** opens one.
- The **gear** in its header opens the settings of the scene you came from. That page's own gear
  opens the frame's page (Wi-Fi, rotation, restart).
- **Back** closes the list again.

## When a board has fewer keys

Most boards don't have all five, so the missing ones are covered like this:

- **No Back key**: press **two keys together** - any two. Press and let go is Back; hold them for
  a second and it's Home. Nobody presses two keys at once by accident, so this never gets in the
  way of anything else.
- **No microphone**: **Action held** is Home.
- **A touch screen**: swipe in from the left edge for back, from the right for forward, up from
  the bottom for Home, and down from the top for the scene's settings. The keys on a touch board
  do what touch can't: the screen and the voice chat.
- **Only one key** (a TRMNL, an ESP32-C3 board): press it for the scene, hold it for Home. In
  Home, each press moves to the next tile and holding the key opens the one it's on.
- **A crown or a knob**: each click is Next or Prev, and pushing it is Action.

## A frame hung another way

Keys with arrows follow the picture. **Next is always the key towards the right or the bottom of
the picture as you see it**, so a reTerminal hung upside down has Next on its right-hand key, and
the arrows printed on the keys point the right way again. Hang it sideways and the two arrow
keys become up and down. The same rotation turns touch, so a swipe and a key always agree. A
knob's clockwise stays clockwise, and Action, Back, Home and Screen do the same however the frame
hangs.

## Change what a key does [#change-what-a-key-does]

**Settings → Button actions** lists what each key does on this frame, whatever scene is showing.
Change a row, add one or clear it there, on the self-hosted backend, in FrameOS Cloud or in the
iOS app. Each row is one press or hold of a key, or a swipe on a touch screen. The rows for the
arrows held and for Back name a key by what it does ("Next key · long press", "Back key") rather
than by its label, because they follow the frame's rotation and stand for two keys pressed
together on a board without a Back key. A press bound there never reaches the scene.

You can still bind a double or triple press of a key there. Only that key then waits about a
third of a second after each press to see whether another follows.

A frame that was set up with an older version's defaults gets the new ones when you update it,
unless you'd changed its button actions yourself: then it keeps yours.

## For scene authors

A scene hears every short press as a `button` event with the key's **role** - `primary` (Action),
`next`, `prev`, `back`, `menu` (Home) or `screen` - so filter on `role`, not on the label printed
on a particular board. Holds are the frame's, so a scene shouldn't rely on hearing one.

- **A scene with buttons on screen** gets the keys as Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter and Escape for free
  ([JSX UIs](/guide/apps#jsx-uis)).
- **A scene with pages or a set of photos** listens for `back` and `forward`. Next and Prev arrive
  as `forward` and `back` on a scene with nothing to press, exactly like the edge swipes, so the
  same scene works with keys and with a finger.
- **A scene written for other keys** - a store scene that listens for `A` and `B` - can be told
  which of your keys are its `A` and `B` with **Map buttons** in the installed scene's menu
  ([details](/guide/scenes#input)).
