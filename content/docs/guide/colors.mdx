title: Colour controls
description: Grade photos for the panel they will hang on - per photo, with the panel's own dither in the preview - and let the frame fit everything else into the panel's range by itself.
---

A six-colour e-ink panel cannot show a photo's white. Its measured white is
closer to `#b2c1c0` - a light grey - and everything brighter than that
quantises to solid white, the error diffusion clips, and the highlights go
flat. Shadows have the same problem at the other end. A photo that looks
right on a laptop screen looks washed out on Spectra 6, and the fix is the
same one a print shop uses: grade for the medium.

FrameOS does this in two layers. **The frame fits every image into the
panel's range on its own**, so a plain slideshow already looks right. And
**each photo can carry its own colour profile**, edited next to the file with
a preview that runs the panel's dither, for the ones you care about.

<Callout title="Where it applies">
  Every runtime: Raspberry Pi, [ESP32](/guide/esp32) and the browser preview, on
  self-hosted and [cloud-managed](/guide/cloud) frames alike. The arithmetic is pinned
  to the same test vectors on the frame and in the editor, so what the preview shows is
  what the panel gets.
</Callout>

## Per photo: Set colors

In the **Assets** panel every image row has a colour button (the sliders icon; a swatch
once the photo has its own profile). It opens an editor with the photo rendered at the
panel's size, dithered to the panel's palette, next to the controls:

- **Adjustments** - *None*, *Auto* or *Custom*. Auto reads the photo's histogram and
  fills in Whites and Blacks so the photo lands inside the range the panel can show. Move
  any slider and it becomes Custom.
- **Light** - exposure (stops), contrast, whites, blacks, saturation.
- **Tones** - a luminance shift and an RGB tint for shadows, midtones and highlights.
- **Hues** - hue, saturation and luminance per range: red, orange, yellow, green, aqua,
  blue, purple, magenta.
- **Dither palette** - the colours the panel dither quantises against, for this photo
  only. Load a measured preset or type your own hex values.

Toggle *Dithered as on the panel* and *Apply adjustments* to compare, and pick the fit
(*Cover* or *Contain*) the scene will use. Previous and next walk the folder; **Save &
next** keeps your sliders and moves on.

Saving writes a small JSON file next to the photo - `cat.jpg` gets `cat.jpg.frameos.json`
- in the frame's assets folder. The file travels with the photo through every control
plane (it is renamed and deleted with it, and hidden from the file tree), and any runtime
applies it whenever it loads that photo. Every key is optional and neutral when missing;
older firmware ignores keys it does not know.

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "colors": {
    "whites": -30,
    "blacks": 10,
    "saturation": 20,
    "hues": { "blue": { "saturation": 30 } },
    "palette": ["#191426", "#b2c1c0", "#c7bb00", "#6b1119", "#18539a", "#2a5531"]
  }
}
```

## Per frame: the automatic fit and a global correction

The frame's **Colors** settings section carries two things that apply to everything it
draws, not just photos with a sidecar:

- **Automatic fit** - *default* (on when the panel dithers to a palette, off on an HDMI or
  LCD display), *on* or *off*. Every image an app draws - a local file, a URL, Immich, an
  AI-generated picture, a JS app's own fetch - has its luminance range mapped onto the
  palette's range right after it is drawn. A photo with its own profile is left alone:
  explicit sliders win.
- **Fine tuning** - the same slider set as the editor, applied once to the whole finished
  canvas, after the fit. The place for "this panel runs a little green".

Both run as 256-entry lookup tables, three lookups per pixel, which is what makes them
affordable on every render of a 13.3" panel on an ESP32. Nothing is resized, proxied or
pre-processed off the frame.

## Per frame: the dither palette

Panels that dither to a palette - the Spectra 6 family - take a **Palette** setting: the six
colours the dither quantises against, as measured on the panel rather than as the
datasheet draws them. The Pi driver reads it at start-up; an ESP32 picks it up on the next
render (firmware 2026.10.4 or newer). A photo's own palette, from the editor above, still
wins for that photo.
