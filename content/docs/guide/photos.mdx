**Photos** is a library of albums that frames play. Upload your pictures once, into albums and
folders, and install an album on a frame: the frame fetches one photo per render through a share
link and decodes it on the device, at whatever size you uploaded it - a 40-megapixel original is
fine, even on an [ESP32](/guide/images).

<Callout title="Self-hosted backend and FrameOS Cloud">
  Photos is a section of the [self-hosted backend](/guide/backend) and of
  [FrameOS Cloud](/guide/cloud) - the **Photos** button in the rail - from 2026.10.13. A
  [standalone frame's](/guide/standalone) admin page has no library of its own, but any frame can
  play an album from either one through its share link. Frames play albums with a JavaScript app
  carried in the scene, so a Pi and an ESP32 behave the same.
</Callout>

## The library

Albums are the top-level folders; inside one, the page is a file explorer - grid or list, nested
folders, breadcrumbs. Drag files in from your desktop (onto the page or onto a folder) or use the
upload button; uploads go up in chunks with a progress bar, at most 250&nbsp;MiB per file. A click
opens a photo in the lightbox, the tick in a tile's corner starts a selection, and **Select all**,
*Select all portrait* and *Select all landscape* are a click away. Rename, move and delete work on a selection,
and the lightbox puts the open photo in the URL, so you can link to it.

## Play an album on a frame

**Install on frame** - on the Photos page, in an album's menu, or under **Add scene → Photos** in a
frame's drawer - makes one scene per album you tick, with:

- **Seconds to show one photo** - the scene's refresh interval;
- **Order** - *Shuffle*, *By name*, *Newest first* or *Oldest first*;
- **Fit** - *Fill the screen* (crop) or *Show the whole photo*;
- which shapes play - **Landscape**, **Portrait** and **Square** photos, so a portrait frame can
  skip the landscapes.

The scene is installed and saved in one step, and the frame keeps its place in the album across
restarts. The same drawer also takes an album link from elsewhere - another backend, or the cloud -
and plays it as is.

**Show on frame** is on every photo's and folder's menu. Pick a frame once, and for the next hour
the menu offers *Show on Kitchen* directly. A folder installs (or reuses) its album scene; a single
photo gets a scene that shows just that photo. Either way the frame switches to it right away.

Or say it: on a frame with a [voice chat](/guide/esp32#voice-chat), *"play the holiday album,
ten seconds each"* installs that album's scene and switches to it (2026.10.20+). The frame's
**Access photo albums** switch decides whether the voice chat may.

## Share links

A frame reads an album through a share link, never with your login. A folder has one link per
frame it plays on (made by **Install on frame**), an optional general link, and any number of
custom links with a name of your choosing ("Grandma"), each listed in the share dialog with the
frame or name it belongs to and when it was last used. **Stop sharing** on one link cuts off only
that frame or person.

Anyone with a link can read that folder - including a friend's frame, or another FrameOS install -
so a scene that plays an album is as private as the link inside it. The cloud's
[scene store](/guide/scene-store) refuses to make such a scene public, the scene AI only ever sees
a placeholder for the link, and frames never log it or draw it on the panel.

## Colors

Every photo can carry its own color profile - exposure, whites and blacks, tones, hues and a dither
palette - edited in the same **Set colors** editor as frame assets, as a panel beside the
full-screen photo. Preview it at full fidelity, as one of your frames shows it (its panel, size,
rotation and automatic correction), or as a panel family would. Frames get each photo's profile
with the photo (FrameOS 2026.10.13+). See [Color controls](/guide/colors).

## Resize

Frames decode any size, but a huge file still costs download time on every render. **Resize** on a
photo's menu (or **Resize all** for a selection) replaces the photo with itself at a size you pick
- your frames' own panel sizes are offered - fitted within, filled and cropped, padded, or
stretched, in its own format or as JPEG, PNG or WebP. Nothing is upscaled unless you ask, and its
colors and place in the album stay.

## Location and other metadata

Photos are stored exactly as you upload them, so a phone photo still carries where it was taken. A
pin on its tile says so, and the lightbox's **Info** panel shows every EXIF field, the coordinates
with a map link, and anything else the file holds - XMP, a motion photo's video. **Remove
location** cuts out just the GPS data; **Remove all metadata** strips the rest while keeping the
pixels, the color profile and the orientation. Both work on a selection too. Worth doing before you
hand out a share link: whoever has it can download the files.

## Storage

**Self-hosted backend.** Photos live in `FRAMEOS_PHOTOS_DIR` if you set it, `/data/photos` on the
Home Assistant add-on, and otherwise in `photos/` next to the database - `/app/db/photos` in
Docker, on the same volume as the rest of the backend. In Docker the Photos page stays off, and
says why, when that folder isn't on a volume, since recreating the container would lose every
photo. Two
uploads of the same photo are stored once. There's no quota beyond your disk.

To keep the bytes in an S3-compatible bucket instead (Cloudflare R2, AWS S3, MinIO), set
`FRAMEOS_PHOTOS_S3_ENDPOINT`, `FRAMEOS_PHOTOS_S3_BUCKET`, `FRAMEOS_PHOTOS_S3_ACCESS_KEY_ID` and
`FRAMEOS_PHOTOS_S3_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`, plus `FRAMEOS_PHOTOS_S3_REGION` and `FRAMEOS_PHOTOS_S3_PREFIX`
if you need them. The bucket stays private: frames and browsers get the bytes through the backend.

**FrameOS Cloud.** The free plan holds 250&nbsp;MB of photos, and the paid plans
([limits and plans](/guide/cloud#limits)) more once your email is verified (a Google sign-in
counts); **Settings → Usage** shows where you stand. The cloud takes JPEG, PNG,
GIF and WebP, and scans every upload against known abuse material before anything is stored.
Photos sit in a private bucket that frames reach only through share links, and a photo you delete -
or every photo, when you delete your account - leaves the bucket, and the backups a month later.

## Scripts and agents

Both MCP servers - [the backend's](/guide/backend#mcp) and [the cloud's](/guide/cloud#developer) -
have the same photo tools: list and fetch photos, upload from a file or a URL, make folders, move,
rename and delete, set a photo's colors, manage share links, and install an album on a frame. Every
tool that changes something takes `confirm: true`, because a frame shows the change on its next
photo.
